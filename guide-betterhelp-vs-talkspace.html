BetterHelp vs Talkspace: Which One Should You Try First?

Published November 2025 · Last updated October 24, 2026 · Written by Paul Paradis, Editor · Educational information, not medical or mental-health advice

Reviewed for educational clarity and safety language by Lisa Lewis, RN, BSN · Independent comparison using public pricing, insurance, privacy, and service information

This is not a brand popularity contest. Start with Talkspace first if your insurance plan, employer EAP, or medication-management need could make it cheaper or more complete. Start with BetterHelp first if you want the larger therapist network (BetterHelp says 31,000+ licensed therapists; Talkspace says 5,600 licensed providers on its home page and 6,000+ clinicians on its About page) and a self-pay subscription that includes messaging, or if your plan is one of the 125+ it takes for therapy. Both companies now offer psychiatry; the practical difference is that Talkspace publishes cash prices for it, while BetterHelp’s psychiatry page centers on insurance and mentions self-pay rates without listing them. The decision to make before signup is this: which platform is more likely to lower your real monthly cost and match your care need before you enter payment information?

The privacy record behind this comparison: BetterHelp agreed in 2023 to pay $7.8 million to settle Federal Trade Commission allegations that it shared email addresses, IP addresses, and answers to health questions with Facebook, Snapchat, and others for advertising. In May 2024 the FTC said about 800,000 people who paid for BetterHelp, or for its sister sites such as Teen Counseling, Faithful Counseling, Pride Counseling, and Regain, between August 1, 2017 and December 31, 2020 would receive refund notices (FTC, May 6, 2024). The final order, approved July 14, 2023, bans BetterHelp from sharing health data for advertising and requires consent before it discloses personal information to certain third parties. According to the FTC’s refund page, the first payments in June 2024 came to nearly $5.2 million, and a second round of more than $2.6 million went out in April 2025. Talkspace was not part of that case.

What to read before you enroll in either one: The settlement covers past conduct, so it says little about how either company handles data today. Open the current privacy policy at signup and look for the sections on advertising and analytics partners, and note whether you are joining through an insurer or employer, since that route can change which privacy rules apply to your records.

Quick Overview

BetterHelp at a Glance

BetterHelp launched in 2013, calls itself the world’s largest therapy service and says it has more than 31,000 licensed therapists (BetterHelp home and About pages, checked October 24, 2026); unlike Talkspace, its core product is a therapy subscription with several ways to reach your therapist. It now also takes insurance for therapy through 125+ plans, which its insurance page says is available nationwide but varies by state, plan, network and therapist, and its psychiatry page offers medication management for adults, mainly through insurance, with sign-up handled by UpLift, which BetterHelp’s parent company, Teladoc Health, bought on April 30, 2025.

Talkspace at a Glance

Talkspace was founded in 2012, per its About page (checked October 24, 2026), and both platforms are online therapy services, but Talkspace also provides online psychiatry, teen therapy, and psychiatric evaluations, while BetterHelp’s own psychiatry service is newer and is presented mainly as an insurance service, though its page says self-pay rates exist. Talkspace is notable for accepting insurance from many major providers and offering employee assistance programs.

Communication Options

BetterHelp Communication

BetterHelp provides multiple ways to communicate with your therapist. A self-pay subscription includes one live session a week plus messaging, while an insurance plan covers one live video session a week and limits messaging to scheduling (BetterHelp FAQ, checked October 24, 2026):

  • Messaging any time (self-pay): Send text messages whenever you like, and audio messages, which BetterHelp’s privacy policy says are transcribed automatically; the FAQ does not describe video messages
  • Live chat: Schedule real-time text conversations (self-pay members only)
  • Phone sessions: Voice calls with your therapist (self-pay members only)
  • Video sessions: Face-to-face video calls, the only live format with insurance

Talkspace says its therapists answer messages within one working day, during their business hours, five days a week (Talkspace pricing FAQ, checked October 24, 2026). BetterHelp’s FAQ does not give a set reply time and says a therapist may not see your message for some time (checked October 24, 2026), so ask your therapist early on when they usually respond. Private messaging can also be useful between scheduled therapy sessions, especially if you want support between online therapy sessions.

Talkspace Communication

Talkspace offers different communication options depending on your plan, and these Talkspace features vary based on how you want to connect with a therapist:

  • Messaging only ($69/week): Message your therapist any time and get responses five days a week; this plan has no live sessions (Talkspace pricing page, checked October 24, 2026)
  • Video + Messaging ($99/week): Includes up to four 30-minute live therapy sessions a month plus unlimited messaging
  • Video + Messaging + Workshops ($109/week): Adds access to weekly live group workshops

The availability of live sessions depends on which plan you choose. With Talkspace therapy, Talkspace therapists generally reply five days a week, and the platform also offers teen therapy and psychiatric evaluations.

Pricing and Insurance

BetterHelp Pricing

  • Cash pay: $70-$100 per week, billed as a subscription; BetterHelp says price depends on factors such as location and therapist availability
  • Insurance: BetterHelp says it accepts 125+ major insurance plans, including Cigna, Aetna, Optum, UnitedHealthcare, and some Blue Cross Blue Shield plans, with copays as low as $15 for eligible members
  • Coverage varies by state, plan, network, deductible, and therapist availability, and insurance may not appear as an option in every signup
  • With insurance, messaging is limited to scheduling because insurers do not pay for messaging time (per BetterHelp's FAQ). An insurance plan covers one live session a week, the average copay is $23, and chat sessions, groups, and some self-help tools may be limited or unavailable
  • BetterHelp’s insurance page says it does not accept Medicaid, Medicare, or TRICARE for therapy
  • Four-week cost without insurance: about $280-$400, since the weekly price is billed weekly or in longer cycles; BetterHelp’s terms list weekly, four-week, and quarterly billing
  • HSA/FSA cards accepted (per BetterHelp)
  • Financial aid: BetterHelp’s FAQ says some members get lower prices through financial aid or discounts (checked October 22, 2026); the signup flow shows whether you qualify
  • AARP members: 30% off the first month of a self-pay subscription for new users, not combined with insurance, plus three free months of the BetterSleep app, per both AARP and BetterHelp (checked October 24, 2026). AARP says BetterHelp pays it a royalty for using its name
  • Cancel anytime without penalty: go to Menu > My Account > Payment Settings > Cancel Membership. Cancel before the renewal date to avoid the next charge; unused live sessions in a billing cycle do not roll over, per BetterHelp’s terms. BetterHelp says you can pause your subscription from the website or app instead, and that refunds are handled case by case

Prices verified on Oct 22, 2026 from betterhelp.com and betterhelp.com/insurance; insurance, cancellation, and AARP details re-checked October 24, 2026. Prices change, so confirm before you pay.

BetterHelp's flat-rate subscription can be a predictable cash-pay option for therapy costs, especially for people who qualify for financial aid.

Talkspace Pricing

  • Individual therapy, cash pay: Messaging only $69/week; Video + messaging $99/week (up to four 30-minute video sessions a month); Video + messaging + workshops $109/week
  • Plans are billed monthly, quarterly, or every six months, per Talkspace, and the longer cycles save 10-20%. Billed monthly, the three plans come to $276, $396, and $436 for four weeks
  • Couples therapy: $109/week, which Talkspace states as $436 a month for four live sessions plus messaging
  • Psychiatry without insurance: $299 for a one-hour initial evaluation, $175 per follow-up, $435 for an evaluation plus one follow-up, or $475 for three follow-ups. There is no subscription, and follow-ups are usually every three months
  • Pause and cancel: self-pay therapy plans can be paused for up to seven days at a time, and the billing date moves back by the paused days; plans through insurance, an EAP, or an employer cannot be paused. Cancel any time from your account; Talkspace’s Help Center says you can still open your therapy room and read past messages after you cancel. Talkspace generally does not refund services already provided, but its support team will review refund requests made within 90 days of a charge
  • Insurance: Talkspace says it is in-network with most major plans, including Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, and TRICARE, and that most insured members have a $0 copay, with an average of $10 per session; it also accepts Medicare in some cases (check eligibility)
  • You may still need to meet your deductible before a copay applies
  • Available through many employer EAP programs, which Talkspace says can mean very low or no cost to employees
  • HSA/FSA eligible

Prices verified on Oct 22, 2026 from talkspace.com/pricing and talkspace.com. Psychiatry, couples, pause, and cancellation details checked October 24, 2026 on the same pricing page and the Talkspace Help Center.

If you plan to use insurance plans and may need psychiatry or medication management, Talkspace is often the better fit between BetterHelp and Talkspace depending on your payment method and medical needs; tools like our online therapy cost calculator for cash pay vs copays can also help you compare likely out-of-pocket costs before you choose.

Insurance Considerations

One of the most significant differences between these platforms is insurance coverage:

  • If you have insurance: check both. Each now bills many major plans, so the cheaper one is whichever is in-network for your plan in your state; Talkspace says most insured members pay $0 per session and puts the average at $10, while BetterHelp advertises copays as low as $15 and an average of $23, and it may also be worth reviewing how workplace mental health benefits and EAP programs fit into your options
  • If paying out-of-pocket: list prices are close ($70-$100/week vs $69-$109/week as of Oct 22, 2026); BetterHelp's subscription may suit you and may include financial aid for those who qualify, while Talkspace is the one that publishes self-pay psychiatry prices if you want medication support without insurance
  • If you have Medicare: BetterHelp’s insurance page says it does not accept Medicare, Medicaid, or TRICARE for therapy, although its psychiatry page lists Medicare among accepted insurers. Talkspace says it accepts Medicare in some cases. Ask either company to confirm before you book
  • If your employer offers EAP: Check if Talkspace is included (may be free)

Therapist Matching and Selection

BetterHelp Matching

  • Answer sign-up questions about your needs and preferences; BetterHelp says it matches you on your objectives, preferences, the issues you are dealing with, and demographics (BetterHelp FAQ and home page, checked October 24, 2026)
  • BetterHelp calls this “smart provider matching” from a pool of over 31,000 providers and says a match takes a few hours or a few days. It does not explain how the matching works, so you cannot tell how much weight your stated preferences get compared with which therapists have openings
  • Can switch therapists at any time at no extra cost, with no limit on switches, if the first match is not the right therapist (BetterHelp billing help article, updated October 13, 2026)
  • Larger network (31,000+ licensed therapists, per BetterHelp, vs 5,600 to 6,000+ for Talkspace) may mean more options for finding a good fit
  • With BetterHelp you are matched to a therapist rather than choosing one from a directory, and its FAQ and home page describe no way to browse therapists before your first match; if a match does not suit you, you ask for a new one
  • This matching model can make mental health counseling easier to access from home instead of relying only on traditional therapy

Talkspace Matching

  • Complete an assessment questionnaire about your areas of concern and preferences; if you plan to use insurance, Talkspace checks your state and coverage as part of matching, so have your insurance card and member ID ready before you begin
  • At sign-up, Talkspace's proprietary algorithm matches you with a provider based on your areas of concern, gender and language preferences, state and coverage, which takes an average of 48 hours; you review a list of matches and pick one yourself when you change providers later. Talkspace's home page puts it more simply, promising access to a licensed therapist in 2 days or sooner, which matches that 48-hour average (Talkspace Help Center article 360000287406 and home page, checked October 24, 2026)
  • Can browse provider profiles, with each provider's background, approach and specialties, in the Talkspace directory and filter by state, specialty, insurance, age group and service type; people in the US signing up with insurance can book a provider straight from the directory (Talkspace Help Center, checked October 24, 2026)
  • Can switch therapists if the first match is not a good fit
  • Some say in who you start with: you can ask Support to match you with a specific provider by name, and if you have browsed the directory you can send Support the names of 3–4 providers you would like, though licensing or insurance limits can rule some out; US members signing up with insurance can book a chosen provider directly (Talkspace Help Center, checked October 24, 2026)

Additional Services

Psychiatry and Medication Management

Talkspace offers psychiatric care through psychiatrists and other licensed psychiatry providers who can prescribe, which its pricing page and therapist-network pages describe as psychiatrists and nurse practitioners, and Talkspace offers psychiatric evaluations online without needing an in-person doctor visit. BetterHelp now offers psychiatry too, through a separate service whose sign-up runs through UpLift: it is for adults 18 and older, is in network with most major insurers with an average copay of $26, does not prescribe controlled substances, and says many people connect with a psychiatric provider within 2.6 days on average (BetterHelp psychiatry page, checked October 24, 2026). BetterHelp therapists themselves do not prescribe. If you may need medication support and plan to pay cash, the distinction is price transparency: Talkspace lists its psychiatry prices, while BetterHelp’s page says it offers self-pay rates “at low costs” but does not list them. Talkspace psychiatry includes:

  • Psychiatric evaluations
  • An initial psychiatric evaluation helps determine a treatment plan and whether prescribed medication is appropriate.
  • Medication prescribing, where appropriate and legally permitted, may be sent to a local pharmacy.
  • Ongoing medication management
  • Prices without insurance of $299 for the initial evaluation and $175 per follow-up, or a copay that Talkspace says averages $15 with insurance

These medication management services are separate from individual therapy and standard talk therapy plans. If you're exploring psychiatry mainly for focus, attention, or impulsivity concerns, it's especially important to understand online ADHD evaluation and medication rules before you start.

Specialized Platforms

BetterHelp operates several sister sites for specific needs:

  • ReGain for couples therapy and relationship support, rather than a built-in family therapist option on the main platform
  • Teen Counseling for adolescents ages 13 to 19, with a parent or guardian’s consent
  • Pride Counseling for LGBTQ+ individuals (on October 24, 2026, pridecounseling.com redirected to betterhelp.com)
  • Faithful Counseling for faith-based therapy (on October 24, 2026, faithfulcounseling.com redirected to betterhelp.com)

Talkspace offers couples therapy and teen therapy within its main platform, so more services stay in one place.

Privacy and Security

Talkspace says it is HIPAA-compliant. BetterHelp’s FAQ points instead to 256-bit encryption and HITRUST r2 certification, and its privacy policy says HIPAA rules apply to members who use insurance. Key considerations:

  • Both say they use encryption for communications (BetterHelp FAQ; Talkspace insurance and psychiatry pages)
  • BetterHelp settled FTC charges over sharing health data for advertising in 2023; Talkspace was not part of that case. Review both current privacy policies
  • BetterHelp lets self-pay members sign up with a nickname, but insurance members must give their legal name; Talkspace lets you set and change a nickname under Account > Login and security
  • Both keep message histories: BetterHelp says you can log in and reread all your therapist’s messages, and Talkspace’s privacy policy lists chat transcripts among the data it collects (which can be helpful or concerning depending on your preferences)

Always review each platform's current privacy policy before signing up.

What the Published Research Says About Each Platform

Both companies point to studies, and both sets of studies have limits worth knowing before you treat them as proof that one platform works better.

BetterHelp. The study BetterHelp cites in its FAQ is a 2019 paper by researchers at UC Berkeley, UCSF and USC, two of whom had worked with BetterHelp as a consultant or staff member, in JMIR mHealth and uHealth (Marcelle et al., 2019). It followed 318 active BetterHelp users, measured depression with the PHQ-9 when they enrolled and again three months later, and found symptoms dropped significantly. People with no earlier experience of face-to-face therapy improved more. The authors found no dose-response effect, meaning people who exchanged more with their therapist did not do measurably better. There was no comparison group, so the study cannot show how much of the improvement came from the platform rather than time or other help.

Talkspace. The strongest Talkspace study is a randomized trial published in JAMA Network Open in October 2025 (Pullmann et al., 2025). University of Washington researchers, with co-authors from Talkspace, randomly assigned 850 adults with depression to message-based or weekly video therapy on the platform for 12 weeks. Depression scores improved about equally: 47.5% of the messaging group and 47.2% of the video group responded, and 31.4% and 30.3% reached remission. People in the video group were more likely to drop out by week 5 (21.3% vs 13.2%), but those who struggled early reported a stronger bond with a video therapist. Unlike the BetterHelp paper, it had a comparison, but only between two formats on one platform, so it does not compare Talkspace with in-person care.

Neither study tells you which company is better. What they do suggest is that the format you will actually stick with matters, and that messaging-heavy plans are a reasonable choice for depression if you use them.

Which Might Be Better For You?

Neither platform is universally "better": the right choice depends on your circumstances:

Online therapy can be as effective as traditional in person therapy for many people, though the best fit depends on the person, the issue, and whether they prefer in person therapy.

If you want flexible, message-based mental health care with a large therapist network, BetterHelp may be a better fit. If you prefer a more structured experience with options that can include psychiatry and medication management, Talkspace may make more sense, and whichever you choose, it can help to think about therapy maintenance and staying well after you feel better so you're planning beyond the first few sessions.

Consider BetterHelp if:

  • You prefer a simple subscription model that, if you self-pay, includes messaging plus one live session a week
  • You want flexibility to switch between communication methods (video, phone, and live chat for self-pay members)
  • You want individual therapy plus extras BetterHelp lists on its home page: 20+ weekly live group seminars and 150+ digital worksheets, some of which may be limited with insurance
  • Your plan is one of the plans BetterHelp accepts, or you are fine with cash pay
  • You value having a large therapist network to find the right fit
  • You need financial assistance (BetterHelp says aid may be available depending on income or employment status), or you are an AARP member who can get 30% off the first self-pay month
  • You want therapy first, and if you later need medication you are comfortable using insurance for BetterHelp’s psychiatry service, which does not prescribe controlled substances

Consider Talkspace if:

  • Your insurance is accepted by Talkspace
  • You may need psychiatric services or medication, especially if your mental health conditions call for psychiatric care or medication management individual therapy coordination
  • Your employer offers Talkspace through EAP
  • You want to browse and select your therapist from options (Talkspace’s directory lets you filter providers, and US members signing up with insurance can book directly)
  • You prefer to pay for only the features you need
  • You want psychiatry with a published cash price ($299 evaluation, $175 follow-up) or want therapy and psychiatry under one account

Common Criticisms of Both Platforms

For balanced consideration, here are common criticisms that have been raised about both platforms:

  • Therapist variability: Quality of experience can vary significantly depending on the individual therapist
  • Response times: Messaging response times can vary and may not meet expectations
  • Cancellation process: Some users report difficulty canceling subscriptions. Both companies say you can cancel any time from account settings; the catch is timing, because a renewal that goes through before you cancel is usually not refunded
  • Not suitable for crisis: Neither platform is appropriate for mental health emergencies
  • Limited specialized treatment: May not be ideal for severe mental illness or specialized therapies

Questions to Ask Yourself

  1. Is my insurance accepted by Talkspace, BetterHelp, or both in my state? (If yes, this could significantly reduce costs)
  2. Do I need medication management? (Both offer psychiatry now; Talkspace lists cash prices, and BetterHelp’s service runs mainly through insurance and does not list self-pay prices)
  3. How important is choosing my own therapist from options?
  4. Do I prefer video sessions, messaging, or both?
  5. What can I afford to pay weekly?
  6. Does my employer offer either platform through EAP?

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between BetterHelp and Talkspace?

The main differences are insurance access and additional services. Talkspace accepts many major insurance plans and offers psychiatry services with medication management, while BetterHelp now also accepts insurance through 125+ plans (per BetterHelp, varies by state, plan, and therapist availability). BetterHelp focuses on therapy and has a very large therapist network, and it now runs a separate insurance-based psychiatry service for adults.

Which is cheaper, BetterHelp or Talkspace?

Without insurance (prices verified on Oct 22, 2026, and re-checked Oct 24, 2026), BetterHelp lists $70-$100 per week, about $280-$400 over four weeks, and Talkspace individual therapy plans list $69, $99, or $109 per week, which is $276, $396, or $436 billed monthly. Both now accept insurance: BetterHelp advertises copays as low as $15 for eligible members, with an average of $23, and Talkspace says most insured members have a $0 copay, with an average of $10. Talkspace may also be free through some employer EAP programs. Always confirm your own price at signup.

Does Talkspace accept insurance?

Yes, Talkspace accepts many major insurance plans including Aetna, Cigna, and Optum. If your insurance is accepted, you may only pay a copay for sessions. Talkspace is also available through many employer EAP programs, which may provide free access to employees.

Can I get medication through BetterHelp or Talkspace?

Yes, both now offer it. Talkspace offers psychiatry services with psychiatric evaluation and medication management for an additional cost: $299 for the initial evaluation and $175 per follow-up without insurance, as of October 24, 2026. BetterHelp offers psychiatry for adults 18 and older through a separate psychiatry service offered through UpLift, which mainly uses insurance and mentions unlisted self-pay rates, with an average copay of $26, and it does not prescribe controlled substances. BetterHelp therapists themselves do not prescribe. If you need medication, compare those two with a dedicated online psychiatry service, and confirm with the provider which medications it can prescribe online.

How does therapist matching work on BetterHelp vs Talkspace?

BetterHelp says it matches you to an available therapist based on your objectives, preferences, the issues you are dealing with, and demographics, usually within a few hours or a few days, with the option to switch at any time; you are matched rather than choosing from a directory. Talkspace's matching asks about your areas of concern, your preferred provider gender and your preferred language, matches you only with providers licensed in your state, and takes an average of 48 hours; if you sign up with insurance in the US, you can also browse its provider directory by state, specialty and insurance and book with a provider directly (Talkspace Help Center, checked October 24, 2026). Both allow switching therapists if needed.

Is BetterHelp or Talkspace better for couples therapy?

For couples therapy, BetterHelp operates a separate sister site called ReGain specifically for relationship counseling. Talkspace offers couples therapy plans directly within their main platform. ReGain says all its therapy is provided by licensed therapists and that you can start alone or invite your partner; neither company says every couples therapist specializes in relationships, so ask about your therapist's couples training.

Are BetterHelp and Talkspace HIPAA compliant?

Talkspace says it is HIPAA-compliant and encrypted. BetterHelp's FAQ cites 256-bit encryption and HITRUST r2 certification, and its privacy policy applies HIPAA to members who use insurance. Both let you go by a nickname: BetterHelp for self-pay members, and Talkspace through Account > Login and security. BetterHelp settled FTC charges in 2023 over sharing health data for advertising, and Talkspace was not part of that case, so reviewing both current privacy policies before signing up is recommended.
