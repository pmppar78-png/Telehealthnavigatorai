Telehealth for Primary Care: Everything You Need to Know

Published December 2025 · Last updated October 24, 2026 · Written and edited by Paul Paradis · Educational information – not medical or mental-health advice

Reviewed for educational clarity and safety language by Lisa Lewis, RN, BSN

988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: Call or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org. Call 911 if you or someone else is in immediate danger. This page is educational and is not a crisis service.

Virtual primary care visits let you see a doctor for many common health concerns, from infections and allergies to prescription refills and lab orders, without leaving home. This guide explores how primary care telehealth works, what conditions can be addressed, and how to make the most of virtual doctor visits.

What Is Primary Care Telehealth?

Primary care telehealth provides access to general medical care through virtual appointments. To understand how telehealth works more broadly, see our beginner's guide. This can include:

  • Urgent care consultations: Same-day care for non-emergency concerns
  • Routine check-ins: Follow-up appointments and wellness visits
  • Prescription management: Refills and medication adjustments
  • Lab result reviews: Discussion of test results
  • Referral coordination: Getting referrals to specialists when needed
  • Health questions: Addressing general health concerns

Conditions That Can Be Addressed via Telehealth

Commonly Treated Conditions

  • Respiratory infections: Cold, flu, sinus infections, bronchitis
  • Allergies: Seasonal allergies, allergic reactions
  • Skin conditions: Rashes, acne, eczema (with photos/video)
  • Urinary issues: UTIs, bladder concerns
  • Digestive problems: Nausea, diarrhea, acid reflux
  • Eye infections: Pink eye, styes
  • Minor injuries: Sprains, strains, minor cuts (assessment and guidance)
  • Headaches: Migraines, tension headaches
  • Insomnia: Sleep difficulties

Ongoing Care Management

  • Chronic conditions: Diabetes, hypertension, asthma (monitoring and management)
  • Mental health: Anxiety, depression (see Mental Health Telehealth Guide)
  • Weight management: Through services like Noom
  • Medication management: Reviewing and adjusting ongoing medications

Prescriptions Available via Telehealth

Many medications can be prescribed through telehealth visits:

  • Antibiotics for infections
  • Antihistamines and allergy medications
  • Blood pressure medications
  • Diabetes medications
  • Birth control (through services like Nurx)
  • Asthma inhalers
  • Anti-nausea medications
  • Some mental health medications

Note: Controlled substances have stricter regulations and may require in-person evaluation in some states. Your provider will advise you on what's appropriate for telehealth.

When Telehealth Is NOT Appropriate

Primary care telehealth has limitations. Seek in-person or emergency care for:

  • Medical emergencies: Chest pain, difficulty breathing, severe bleeding
  • Severe symptoms: High fever unresponsive to treatment, severe pain
  • Physical examination needs: Conditions requiring hands-on assessment
  • Lab work: Blood draws, urine samples, imaging
  • Procedures: Stitches, injections, biopsies
  • Complex conditions: New or worsening symptoms requiring comprehensive evaluation

Emergency Warning: If you are experiencing a medical emergency, call 911 or go to your nearest emergency room. Telehealth cannot provide emergency care.

How Primary Care Telehealth Works

Before Your Appointment

  1. Choose a service: Select a telehealth platform or use your regular doctor's virtual option
  2. Create an account: Provide personal and insurance information
  3. Describe your concern: Complete intake questions about your symptoms
  4. Schedule or request visit: Book an appointment or request on-demand care
  5. Prepare information: Have your medication list, symptoms, and questions ready
  6. Test your technology: Test your camera, microphone, and internet first. See our preparing for your virtual visit guide for a complete setup checklist

During Your Appointment

  • Connect with your provider via video or phone
  • Describe your symptoms and concerns
  • Answer questions about your health history
  • Show affected areas if relevant (skin, throat, etc.)
  • Receive assessment and recommendations
  • Get prescriptions sent to your pharmacy if needed

After Your Appointment

  • Review visit summary and instructions
  • Pick up any prescriptions from your pharmacy
  • Schedule follow-up if needed
  • Contact provider if symptoms worsen

Types of Primary Care Telehealth Services

On-Demand Urgent Care

See a doctor quickly for urgent but non-emergency concerns:

  • Teladoc - 24/7 access to doctors
  • MDLive - on-demand virtual urgent care
  • Doctor On Demand - quick access to physicians
  • Amwell - virtual doctor visits

Virtual Primary Care Practices

Ongoing care with a consistent provider:

  • PlushCare - virtual primary care with a dedicated doctor
  • K Health - AI-assisted primary care
  • HealthTap - virtual primary care membership

Cash-Pay Options

Affordable care without insurance:

  • Sesame - transparent cash pricing
  • Many platforms offer uninsured rates

Cost of Primary Care Telehealth

Costs vary based on service type and insurance. For a detailed breakdown, see our guide on what virtual doctor visits cost.

  • With insurance: Typically $0-$75 copay depending on your plan
  • Without insurance: Usually $50-$100+ per visit
  • Subscription services: Monthly fees for unlimited or discounted visits
  • Employer benefits: Many employers offer free or reduced-cost telehealth

Prescription savings through GoodRx can help reduce medication costs after your visit.

Tips for Effective Telehealth Visits

  • Prepare your space: Find a quiet, well-lit, private location
  • Gather information: Have your medication list and relevant health records
  • List your questions: Write down what you want to discuss
  • Describe symptoms clearly: Include when they started, severity, and what makes them better or worse
  • Take photos: If relevant, photograph skin conditions or affected areas before the visit
  • Be honest: Share complete information about your health and lifestyle
  • Take notes: Write down the provider's recommendations
  • Ask for clarification: Don't hesitate to ask questions if something isn't clear

Telehealth and Your Regular Doctor

You can often use telehealth while maintaining care with your existing physician:

  • Many primary care practices now offer their own telehealth options
  • Use urgent care telehealth for after-hours or weekend needs
  • Request that telehealth providers send records to your regular doctor
  • Continue in-person visits for annual physicals and comprehensive care

What video cannot do

What people pay

Original Medicare in 2026: standard Part B premium $202.90 a month, deductible $283, then 20% coinsurance of the approved amount (CMS fact sheet). Home and any-location telehealth run through December 31, 2027 (CMS FAQ, updated October 8, 2026). Cash urgent-care video prices are set by each company and change. Read the price on that company's page before you start the visit. A posted cash price is not your Medicare coinsurance.

Who primary care by video suits

It works best for adults who already have a regular clinician and need something routine: a blood pressure medicine adjusted after home readings, a refill, lab results explained, or a rash photographed in good light. It suits people far less well when they have a new symptom that needs hands-on examination, such as abdominal pain, a breast lump or a child's earache. Those visits often end with "come in anyway," and then you have paid for two visits.

How a typical visit runs

You check in on the portal, confirm the state you are in (the clinician must be licensed there), and answer a short intake. The clinician takes a history and may ask you to press on a spot, take your own pulse or hold the camera to your throat. Orders for labs or imaging go to a local site, and the visit note should appear in your record within a day or two. Medicare pays for these visits from home through December 31, 2027, with the usual Part B deductible and 20% coinsurance.

Risks to weigh

  • Missed findings: no stethoscope, no exam of the belly, no reflexes. Tell the clinician plainly if something feels worse than you can describe.
  • Antibiotics: a study in Pediatrics (Ray et al., 2019) found children seen through direct-to-consumer telemedicine received antibiotics for respiratory infections at 52% of visits, against 42% at urgent care and 31% with their own primary care doctor. Ask whether a prescription is really needed.
  • Fragmented records: a one-off app visit rarely reaches your regular doctor unless you forward the note.

Frequently asked questions

What conditions can be treated through primary care telehealth?

Common conditions treated via telehealth include respiratory infections like cold, flu, and sinus infections; allergies; skin conditions with photos or video; urinary issues like UTIs; digestive problems; eye infections; minor injuries; headaches and migraines; and chronic condition management for diabetes, hypertension, and asthma.

Can I get prescriptions through a telehealth visit?

Yes, many medications can be prescribed through telehealth including antibiotics, antihistamines, blood pressure medications, diabetes medications, birth control, asthma inhalers, and some mental health medications. Controlled substances have stricter regulations and may require in-person evaluation in some states.

When is telehealth NOT appropriate for primary care?

Seek in-person or emergency care for medical emergencies like chest pain or difficulty breathing, severe symptoms like high unresponsive fever, conditions requiring physical examination, lab work or imaging, procedures like stitches or injections, and complex conditions requiring comprehensive evaluation.

How much does primary care telehealth cost?

With insurance, telehealth typically costs $0-$75 copay depending on your plan. Without insurance, visits usually cost $50-$100 or more. Subscription services offer monthly fees for unlimited or discounted visits. Many employers offer free or reduced-cost telehealth as a benefit.

What should I prepare before a telehealth appointment?

Find a quiet, well-lit, private location. Gather your medication list and relevant health records. Write down your questions and describe symptoms clearly including when they started, severity, and what makes them better or worse. Take photos of any skin conditions beforehand and test your technology.

What are the best primary care telehealth platforms?

For on-demand urgent care, consider Teladoc, MDLive, Doctor On Demand, or Amwell for 24/7 access. For ongoing care with a consistent provider, PlushCare, K Health, and HealthTap offer virtual primary care. For affordable cash-pay options, Sesame offers transparent pricing without insurance.
