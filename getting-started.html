Getting Started with Telehealth

By Paul Paradis · Published April 18, 2026 · Editor bio

Most first-timers tell us the hardest part of telehealth was not the therapy itself. It was the hour before the first call: fumbling with the video link, wondering what to say, second-guessing the whole plan. This page is the shelf I'd hand a friend who just booked an appointment for next week and wants to know what to do between now and then.

Read these in order if you're brand-new. Skip around if you're not.

Before your first visit

The guides in this group cover the work you do before anyone signs into the meeting. How to pick the right platform for your situation, how to confirm the clinician you're seeing is real and licensed, and how to make sure your tech works so the first session is about therapy and not about audio settings.

  • Preparing for Your Appointment
  • Technical Setup Guide
  • Evaluating Therapist Credentials
  • How to Choose Online Therapy

Early sessions

First meetings are almost always partly administrative: intake forms, history, what you hope to get out of therapy. These guides walk through what actually happens in the first few sessions, how to build rapport over video (which is different from in person), and what to do if you're coming to online care after years of in-person work.

  • Your First Therapy Session
  • Building a Provider Relationship
  • Switching to Online Therapy

Mental-health foundations

Therapy works better when the rest of your life is at least mildly cooperating. This group is less about telehealth and more about the habits clinicians consistently flag: sleep, movement, nutrition, journaling, and a baseline wellness routine you can actually stick to. These guides are supplements to professional care, not replacements.

  • Mental Wellness Routine
  • Journaling for Mental Health
  • Meditation Apps
  • Exercise & Mental Health
  • Nutrition & Mental Health
  • Preventative Mental Health

Start here if…

  • You haven't picked a platform yet: start with Telehealth Platforms Compared and How to Choose Online Therapy.
  • You have an appointment on the calendar and it's stressing you out: read Preparing for Your Appointment and do the tech check tonight.
  • You've been in therapy before but never online: Switching to Online Therapy covers what changes and what doesn't.
  • You're not ready for therapy yet: start with Mental Wellness Routine and Self-Help Resources.
