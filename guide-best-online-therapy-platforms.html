More therapists can now bill Medicare: Since January 1, 2024, Medicare Part B has paid for services from enrolled marriage and family therapists and mental health counselors, two license types that could not bill Medicare independently before then; Part B pays them 75% of what a clinical psychologist is paid (CMS). For anyone 65 or older, that widened the pool of therapists who may appear as in-network on insurance-billed platforms, though each clinician still has to enroll with Medicare individually.

Marketplace plans include therapy by law: HealthCare.gov lists psychotherapy and counseling among the essential health benefits every Marketplace plan must cover, and parity rules bar those plans from applying copays, visit caps, or prior-authorization rules to mental health care that are stricter than the ones used for medical care (HealthCare.gov). If you bought coverage there, an insurance-billed platform is usually worth checking before a cash subscription.

Overview of Major Platforms

BetterHelp

BetterHelp calls itself the world’s largest therapy service and says it has more than 31,000 licensed therapists, connecting self-pay members with them through messaging, live chat, phone, and video sessions. The platform's strength is breadth: a very large therapist network, matching it says takes a few hours to a few days, and easy switching when fit isn't right. Its tradeoff is that its insurance option is newer and narrower than its self-pay subscription: an insurance plan covers one live session a week, messaging is limited to scheduling, and which plans it takes depends on your state, plan, network and therapist, even though its insurance page calls the option available nationwide, so check eligibility during signup.

  • Communication options: Messaging, live chat, phone, and video sessions for self-pay members; with insurance, live video only, with messaging limited to scheduling
  • Pricing model: Weekly subscription, $70-$100/week cash pay, or insurance copays from $15 for eligible members (BetterHelp site, October 22, 2026)
  • Insurance: In network with 125+ health plans, available nationwide but varying by state, plan, network and therapist, with an average copay of $23 per session; providers on BetterHelp do not directly accept Medicaid, Medicare, or TRICARE for therapy (BetterHelp insurance page, checked October 24, 2026)
  • Psychiatry: A separate service for adults 18+, offered through UpLift and run mainly through insurance (its page mentions self-pay rates but lists none), average copay $26, no controlled substances (BetterHelp psychiatry page, checked October 24, 2026)
  • Therapist network: Very large licensed therapist network: 31,000+ licensed therapists, per BetterHelp’s home page
  • Matching process: BetterHelp matches you to a therapist based on your needs, preferences and demographics (it calls this smart provider matching, without saying how it works), with unlimited free switching
  • Financial assistance: Offers financial aid for qualifying individuals; BetterHelp says eligibility is often based on income

Potential considerations: Subscription-based pricing may not suit everyone; insurance eligibility can vary.

Talkspace

Talkspace offers online therapy and psychiatry services, with a focus on both messaging-based therapy and live video sessions. Among the major subscription brands, Talkspace stands out for actual in-network insurance billing with several major insurers and EAPs, plus a separate psychiatry service for medication management.

  • Communication options: Messaging, video, and audio sessions
  • Pricing model: Multiple plan tiers; starts at $69/week for messaging only, then $99 and $109 with live sessions
  • Insurance: Accepts many major insurance plans, including Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, Optum, TRICARE and some Medicare plans
  • Psychiatry: Offers psychiatric evaluation and medication management
  • Matching process: Questionnaire-based matching by its proprietary algorithm, averaging 48 hours (Talkspace Help Center article 360000287406), plus a provider directory you can browse
  • Employee programs: Offered through many employers' EAP programs

Potential considerations: Plan structures can be complex; some features require higher-tier plans.

Calmerry

Calmerry is an online therapy platform that emphasizes affordability and accessibility. Its regular prices of $57–$90 a week sit at the lower end of subscription pricing for online therapy, and two of its four plans combine messaging with live video; the others are messaging only or video only.

  • Communication options: Text messaging and 30-minute live video sessions, depending on plan
  • Pricing model: Subscription-based, billed monthly; $57–$90 a week at regular price
  • Insurance: Does not accept insurance; Calmerry says it can give you a receipt to seek reimbursement
  • Therapist network: Licensed therapists with various specializations
  • Features: Mood tracker, guided journaling, and a library of 100+ resources, free even without a subscription; therapists may assign worksheets as homework
  • Trial period: No free therapy plan or trial; Calmerry offers a single 60-minute video session so you can try it before subscribing

Potential considerations: Calmerry does not publish its therapist count (it says 70K+ clients), so its network size can’t be compared; no insurance option.

7 Cups

7 Cups offers a unique model combining free peer support with paid professional therapy services. The free tier is genuinely free and can be useful as a low-pressure first step, but it is peer support, not licensed therapy. The paid therapy tier connects you to licensed clinicians.

  • Free tier: Text-based support from trained volunteer listeners
  • Paid therapy: Licensed therapists available for a monthly fee; 7 Cups says it will match you or you can pick from its directory
  • Communication options: Daily messaging with your therapist Monday to Friday, or a weekly 45-minute live video or audio session, which 7 Cups calls new
  • Community: Chat rooms, forums and scheduled group support sessions, including a teen community for ages 13–17
  • Self-help tools: Free resources including growth paths and exercises
  • Accessibility: Good option for those exploring mental health support

Potential considerations: Peer supporters are not licensed therapists; its therapy page does not mention insurance or list prices, so check the cost before you sign up.

Online-Therapy.com

Online-Therapy.com specializes in Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) with a structured approach to treatment. The CBT workbook material is genuinely structured and useful for users who want a self-directed framework to follow alongside therapist sessions.

  • Therapeutic approach: CBT-focused with structured program
  • Communication options: Unlimited messaging on every plan, plus 45-minute live sessions by video, voice, or text chat in your browser on the $90 and $120 plans; the therapist does not call your phone
  • Pricing model: Subscription-based with multiple tiers, $60, $90 or $120 a week, billed monthly
  • Insurance: Does not accept insurance directly; it gives itemized receipts for reimbursement claims
  • Tools included: Worksheets, journal, activity plan, yoga videos
  • Structure: 8-section CBT program with 25 worksheets and therapist replies Monday to Friday

Potential considerations: CBT focus may not suit all needs; structured approach may feel restrictive for some.

Factors to Consider When Choosing

Your Budget

  • If cost is a primary concern, consider 7 Cups' free peer support or platforms with financial assistance
  • If you have insurance, Talkspace may offer coverage options
  • Compare total monthly costs including any additional features you need

Communication Preferences

  • If you prefer video sessions, most platforms offer this option
  • If you prefer text-based communication, 7 Cups and messaging-focused plans may suit you
  • If you want flexibility, BetterHelp and Talkspace offer multiple communication methods

Type of Support Needed

  • For medication management, consider platforms with psychiatry services like Talkspace
  • For structured CBT treatment, Online-Therapy.com specializes in this approach
  • For general support and exploring therapy, larger platforms offer more variety

Specific Needs

  • For couples therapy, consider specialized platforms like ReGain
  • For teen therapy, consider Teen Counseling
  • For specific conditions, look for therapists with relevant specializations

Questions to Ask Before Signing Up

  1. What is the total cost, and what is included in that price?
  2. Can I switch therapists if the first match isn't right?
  3. What happens if I need to cancel or pause my subscription?
  4. Are the therapists licensed in my state?
  5. What communication methods are available?
  6. How quickly can I expect responses from my therapist?
  7. Is there financial assistance available if I need it?
  8. What is the platform's privacy policy?

Platforms Worth Knowing: Quick Summary

Each platform below targets a different type of user. Use this as a fast skim before reading the detailed profiles.

  • BetterHelp: Calls itself the world’s largest therapy service (31,000+ licensed therapists). Weekly fee, messaging plus one weekly live session for self-pay members. Bills 125+ insurance plans, with coverage varying by state and plan, and runs a separate insurance-based psychiatry service.
  • Talkspace: Subscription with multiple tiers; in-network with several major insurers; offers psychiatry.
  • Brightside: Depression and anxiety care for ages 13 and up, with therapy and prescribing; says it accepts most major insurance, including Medicare and Medicaid.

  • Headway: Insurance-first directory of 80,000+ therapists and psychiatric providers that handles insurance billing, per Headway.

  • Online-Therapy.com: Subscription with structured CBT program plus daily therapist replies, and weekly live sessions on the higher plans.
  • Calmerry: Lower-cost subscription option with messaging, video, or both, depending on plan.

  • Teen Counseling: Teen-focused therapy for ages 13–19 on subscription (BetterHelp family, per the FTC).

  • Alma / Sondermind / Rula: Insurance-first services similar to Headway; all three also offer medication management, per their own sites.

Detailed Platform Profiles

Each profile below covers who the platform is for, format, pricing model, insurance, prescribing, strengths, and gaps. Always verify current pricing and policies on the platform's own site.

Headway

  • Who it's for: Anyone with insurance who wants to find an in-network therapist quickly.
  • Format: Therapists and psychiatric providers who bill your insurance through Headway, with in-person and online sessions in 57+ languages, per Headway.
  • Pricing model: Per-session, billed to insurance.
  • Insurance: Headway says it partners with 100+ insurance plans; its home page names Oxford, UnitedHealthcare and Oscar, and you can check your own plan before booking.
  • Prescribing: Yes; Headway’s network includes psychiatric providers, such as psychiatric nurse practitioners, alongside therapists.
  • Strengths: Cost estimate from your insurance details before booking, and billing only after the session; deep network of 80,000+ licensed providers, per Headway.
  • Gaps: Built around in-network insurance; therapist quality varies as it does in any directory; Headway filters matches from your preferences, but choosing among them is on you.

Grow Therapy

  • Who it's for: Insurance users who want a guided matching experience.
  • Format: Virtual and in-person therapy; you message your provider through the client portal about appointments.
  • Pricing model: Per-session, billed to insurance.
  • Insurance: Grow says more than 230 million people are covered and sessions average $21 with insurance; it offers a cost estimate before you book.
  • Prescribing: Grow lists medication support and has prescribers on its network; check availability by state.
  • Strengths: Cost estimate up front, HSA/FSA-eligible costs, and matching on your goals and preferences, including condition-specific specialists.
  • Gaps: Network depth varies by state and insurer.

Rula

  • Who it's for: Insurance users who want speed; Rula says you can meet a provider as soon as tomorrow.
  • Format: Live video therapy and psychiatry, with some providers also seeing people in person.
  • Pricing model: Per-session, billed to insurance, averaging $15 per session; $150 per therapy session without insurance.
  • Insurance: 100+ commercial plans, including Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, Optum and UnitedHealthcare, plus Medicare, Medicare Advantage and Medicaid in many states.
  • Prescribing: Yes; Rula offers psychiatry, $225 for a 60-minute intake and $175 per follow-up without insurance.
  • Strengths: Speed of matching; combined therapy and meds.
  • Gaps: Kaiser members need a referral, and Medicare and Medicaid are accepted in many states, not all.

Alma

  • Who it's for: People who want a curated directory and detailed therapist bios.
  • Format: Clinicians who see people in person, virtually, or both; you can book a short consultation first.
  • Pricing model: Per-session; insured clients pay $20 on average, per Alma, and therapists also take out-of-network clients at their own rates, many on a sliding scale.
  • Insurance: Most therapists on Alma accept insurance; pick your plan in its directory to see who is in network.
  • Prescribing: Yes; Alma says its clinicians include medication management.
  • Strengths: Detailed therapist profiles; individual, couples, family, child and teen therapy; sliding-scale out-of-network care.
  • Gaps: Alma does not publish a network size on its site, so compare it with Headway by searching for your own plan.

Sondermind

  • Who it's for: Insurance users, including people on Medicare, Medicare Advantage or TRICARE, who want a matched therapist or psychiatry provider.
  • Format: Virtual or in-person therapy, usually about 60 minutes weekly or every other week, plus an app.
  • Pricing model: Per-session, billed to insurance, with self-pay rates also offered.
  • Insurance: Most major plans, plus Medicare, Medicare Advantage and TRICARE; coverage depends on your plan.
  • Prescribing: Yes, through psychiatry providers (MD, DO or PMHNP).
  • Strengths: Technology-based matching on your criteria; provider credentials shown on profiles.
  • Gaps: SonderMind says it offers care in all 50 states, but which providers you can see still depends on your plan.

BetterHelp

  • Who it's for: People who want a predictable weekly subscription with messaging plus video; people without insurance.
  • Format: For self-pay members, asynchronous messaging plus one weekly live session (video, phone, or chat); with insurance, one live video session a week.
  • Pricing model: Subscription at $70–$100 per week without insurance (checked October 22, 2026).
  • Insurance: Now accepts insurance for eligible members per its site; HSA/FSA also accepted. Verify your plan before signup.
  • Prescribing: Not by its therapists. BetterHelp’s separate psychiatry service, run through UpLift, offers medication management for adults 18+ through insurance, with an average copay of $26, and does not prescribe controlled substances (BetterHelp psychiatry page, checked October 24, 2026).
  • Strengths: Largest therapist network named here (31,000+, per BetterHelp). Matching in a few hours to a few days. Free, unlimited therapist switching.
  • Gaps: Subscription cost adds up if your plan would otherwise give you a low copay elsewhere (insurance-billed services here report averages of $15 to $30 a session); therapy quality varies as in any large network.

Talkspace

  • Who it's for: People whose insurance accepts Talkspace, or who want messaging-style therapy plus optional psychiatry.
  • Format: Messaging plus optional live sessions by video, audio, or chat; psychiatry available separately.
  • Pricing model: Subscription tiers; in-network billing where available.
  • Insurance: In-network with several major insurers including TRICARE, some Medicare Advantage plans, and EAPs.
  • Prescribing: Yes, psychiatry is a distinct service.
  • Strengths: Combined therapy and meds; insurance billing for many users.
  • Gaps: Plan tiers can be confusing; some features require higher-tier plans.

Brightside

  • Who it's for: People 13 and older with depression, anxiety or related conditions wanting a focused therapy + meds approach.
  • Format: 1:1 video sessions plus anytime messaging, self-guided lessons and weekly check-ins.
  • Pricing model: Self-pay plans of $95 a month for psychiatry, $299 for therapy, or $349 for both; with insurance, a $25 average copay per session.
  • Insurance: Brightside says it accepts most major insurance, including Medicare and Medicaid.
  • Prescribing: Yes; psychiatrists or psychiatric-mental health nurse practitioners.
  • Strengths: Treatment built on cognitive and behavioral therapy with progress tracking; clear focus on depression and anxiety, including care for elevated suicide risk.
  • Gaps: Less broad than general-purpose platforms, and Brightside says online care is not a good fit for psychosis, severe substance use, or medically unstable eating disorders.

Talkiatry

  • Who it's for: People who specifically need a psychiatrist (not therapist) and want to use insurance.
  • Format: Online psychiatry; Talkiatry says first visits are generally 60 minutes or more.
  • Pricing model: Per-visit, billed to insurance.
  • Insurance: Talkiatry says all its clinicians are in network with major insurers and that most patients pay around $30 a visit.
  • Prescribing: Yes, Talkiatry says its psychiatrists are fully licensed to prescribe; it does not treat primary substance use disorders.
  • Strengths: Psychiatrists it vets and hires itself, averaging 10 years of clinical experience; insurance-billed; care for children ages 5 and up as well as adults.
  • Gaps: Not for therapy alone; Talkiatry promises appointments in days but does not publish an average wait, so ask when you book.

Online-Therapy.com

  • Who it's for: Self-directed people who want structured CBT material plus a therapist.
  • Format: Subscription with worksheets, journal, and unlimited messaging on every plan; weekly live sessions (video, voice or text) on the $90 and $120 plans.
  • Pricing model: Subscription tiers.
  • Insurance: No.
  • Prescribing: No.
  • Strengths: The CBT program itself is genuinely structured and useful.
  • Gaps: CBT-only frame may not fit every concern; cash-pay only, though it offers financial aid at checkout.

Calmerry

  • Who it's for: Budget-conscious users wanting subscription therapy.
  • Format: Messaging, 30-minute video sessions, or both, depending on plan.
  • Pricing model: Subscription, $57–$90 a week at regular price, at the lower end of the range.
  • Insurance: No.
  • Prescribing: No.
  • Strengths: Affordability; free mood tracker, guided journaling and resource library.
  • Gaps: No published therapist count, so you can’t compare its network with the largest platforms.

ReGain

  • Who it's for: Couples (or individuals working on relationship issues) who want subscription messaging-plus-video.
  • Format: Subscription; you can start alone or invite your partner to a joint account, where all written messages are visible to both of you.
  • Pricing model: Weekly fee of $70–$100, the same range BetterHelp lists.
  • Insurance: No.
  • Strengths: Low-friction couples therapy without insurance hassle.
  • Gaps: ReGain does not take insurance, even though Talkspace says couples therapy is covered by many insurance plans and BetterHelp lists couples therapy among services that may be covered.

Ours

  • Who it's for: Couples, including engaged couples who want premarital counseling, who prefer live sessions with a couples specialist over a messaging subscription.
  • Format: 50-minute video sessions with a hand-matched licensed couples therapist, plus free self-guided check-in tools between sessions. You can switch therapists at no extra cost.
  • Pricing model: Pay per session or buy a package. Prices quoted for Ours around the web are undated and do not agree with each other, so ask Ours for a current quote.
  • Insurance: Not mentioned on its site; ask before booking.
  • Strengths: Every therapist works with couples, sessions run longer than many telehealth visits, and there is no long subscription to commit to.
  • Gaps: There is no messaging-based plan between sessions.
  • Before you book: Ours's website was not loading when we last checked (October 24, 2026), so confirm the session length, format, prices and switching policy directly with Ours before you book.

Teen Counseling

  • Who it's for: Parents of teens (13–19) who want subscription therapy with parental involvement.
  • Format: Subscription with separate parent and teen channels.
  • Pricing model: Weekly fee of $70–$100; a parent or guardian signs up and gives consent; HSA/FSA cards accepted.
  • Insurance: Unclear. On October 24, 2026 Teen Counseling’s FAQ listed only self-pay prices and HSA/FSA cards and said nothing about insurance, while BetterHelp’s insurance page listed teen therapy among the services that may be covered by insurance. Get an answer in writing before you sign up.
  • Strengths: Designed for the teen-with-parent dynamic; parents can meet the therapist separately but cannot read the teen’s messages, except where the therapist must share safety concerns.
  • Gaps: Usually paid out of pocket, and the teen has to actually engage.

Decision Tree: Which Platform Should You Pick?

Match yourself to the situation that fits best:

If you have insurance and want to use it

Start with Headway or Grow Therapy for therapy. If you also need medication, add Talkiatry for psychiatry. Each verifies your benefits before booking. See does insurance cover online therapy? for the verification walkthrough.

If you want messaging-style therapy and predictable cost

BetterHelp is the broadest option. Calmerry and Online-Therapy.com are alternatives at lower price points. Talkspace is a fit if your insurance happens to cover it.

If you need both therapy and medication in one place

Brightside if you want it bundled. Or split it: an insurance-billed therapist via Headway plus a Talkiatry psychiatrist. The split approach can cost less, depending on your copays, and Talkiatry staffs psychiatrists, while Brightside’s prescribers may be psychiatrists or nurse practitioners; see best online psychiatry services.

If you specifically want couples therapy

ReGain for an ongoing subscription with messaging, Ours if you want live sessions with a couples specialist and pay as you go. Insurance may cover couples therapy on some plans (Talkspace says many plans cover it), but ReGain does not bill insurance, so check your plan.

If you're looking for teen therapy

Teen Counseling is a teen-specific platform for ages 13–19, run by the BetterHelp family. For deeper insurance-billed teen care, look for in-network adolescent specialists on Headway, Grow Therapy, or Rula.

If you need low-cost or have no insurance

Calmerry on the subscription side. Open Path Collective and FQHCs on the in-person/sliding-scale side. 7 Cups for free peer support (not licensed therapy). See our low-cost telehealth guide.

If you have a specific condition

For panic, anxiety, OCD, ADHD, or trauma, the platform matters less than the therapist's specialty. Use the insurance-first directories and filter by specialty. Pricing Reality: What People Actually Pay

For cash-pay subscription platforms, regular prices run about $57–$120 per week, with messaging-only tiers cheapest and plans with weekly video at the top (October 22, 2026). For insurance-billed platforms, the averages the companies publish run from $10 to about $30 per session (Talkspace $10, Rula $15, Alma $20, Grow $21, BetterHelp $23, Brightside $25, Talkiatry about $30), and you may pay the full negotiated rate until your deductible is met. Online psychiatry runs about $225–$500 cash-pay for the intake and $100–$300 for medication-management follow-ups, varying by provider and state: Rula lists $225 for a first visit, Talkspace and Doctor On Demand list $299, MDLIVE lists up to $299, Blossom Health lists $300, and Talkiatry's cost guide puts an uninsured evaluation at $300–$500. Membership pricing depends on if you're getting therapy, prescribing, or both: Brightside, for example, lists $95 a month for psychiatry alone, $299 for therapy alone, and $349 a month for both (checked October 22 and October 24, 2026). Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best online therapy platform overall?

There isn't one. The best platform depends on your insurance, your need for medication, your budget, and what format works for you. For most insurance users, Headway, Grow Therapy, Rula, Alma, or Sondermind are strong starting points. For predictable cash-pay subscription, BetterHelp is the broadest option. Among the best-known names, BetterHelp has a very large therapist network, Talkspace accepts many insurance plans, Calmerry offers competitive pricing, 7 Cups provides free peer support, and Online-Therapy.com specializes in CBT.

Is online therapy as effective as in-person therapy?

Research supports that online therapy is comparable in effectiveness to in-person therapy for many common conditions including anxiety, depression, and PTSD, especially when delivered by a licensed clinician using evidence-based methods. It is not appropriate for crisis situations or severe mental illness requiring intensive monitoring.

Which online therapy platforms accept insurance?

Headway, Grow Therapy, Rula, Alma, and Sondermind are insurance-first for therapy, and Talkiatry is insurance-first for psychiatry. Talkspace says it is in network with most major plans, and Brightside says it accepts most major insurance, including Medicare and Medicaid. BetterHelp says it is in network with 125+ health plans, but coverage depends on your state and plan, so verify it directly before signup. Calmerry, Online-Therapy.com and ReGain are cash-pay, and Teen Counseling's FAQ lists only self-pay prices.

Can I switch therapists on most online platforms?

Yes. BetterHelp, Talkspace, Calmerry, Online-Therapy.com, 7 Cups, and Rula say you can switch therapists, and on directories like Headway and Grow Therapy you can book someone else, if the initial match is not a good fit; BetterHelp allows unlimited switches at no extra cost, while other platforms may have different policies. Use this: if the fit feels wrong after a few sessions, switching usually costs less than quitting.

Which online therapy platform is cheapest?

With insurance: whichever insurance-first platform shows the lowest copay for your plan. Without insurance: subscription platforms list roughly $57–$120 per week at regular price; community options (Open Path, FQHCs, training clinics) can be cheaper still. See low-cost telehealth options.

Do online therapy platforms offer psychiatry and prescribing too?

Some do. Talkspace, Brightside, Talkiatry, Rula, SonderMind and Alma include psychiatric prescribing or medication management, and Headway and Grow Therapy list psychiatric providers too. BetterHelp therapists do not prescribe, but BetterHelp now runs a separate psychiatry service, mainly insurance-based, for medication management. Online-Therapy.com and ReGain say their therapists cannot prescribe medication, and Calmerry and Teen Counseling do not list prescribing among their services.

How do I know if a therapist is actually licensed?

Reputable platforms require state licensure verification before allowing therapists on the platform. You can independently verify by searching your state's licensing board with the therapist's name and license number. See how to verify therapist credentials.

Is BetterHelp covered by insurance?

Sometimes. As of October 24, 2026, BetterHelp says it is in network with 125+ health plans, with copays as low as $15 for eligible members, but coverage depends on your state and plan, so do not assume it from older comparison pages. Some users use HSA or FSA dollars, and some may see plan-specific eligibility. Confirm directly with BetterHelp and your insurer before signing up.

How much does online therapy cost?

As of October 22, 2026, BetterHelp lists $70-$100 per week and Talkspace $69-$109 per week without insurance; Calmerry ($57-$90) and Online-Therapy.com ($60-$120) are similar at regular price. Costs vary based on location, plan type, and whether you use insurance. Talkspace and BetterHelp both accept some insurance plans, and BetterHelp also offers financial aid to some members who qualify.

Does insurance cover online therapy?

Some online therapy platforms accept insurance. Talkspace says it is in network with most major plans, and BetterHelp says it is in network with 125+ health plans, available nationwide but varying by state and plan; for both, coverage depends on your plan and location. Always check with both your insurance provider and the platform to verify coverage before signing up.

What communication options are available with online therapy?

Online therapy platforms offer various communication methods including video sessions, phone calls, live chat, and text messaging. BetterHelp includes video, phone and live chat for self-pay members but only video with insurance, while Talkspace requires its $99 or $109 plans for live video sessions.

Are there free online therapy options?

7 Cups offers free peer support from trained volunteer listeners, though this is not professional therapy. Many platforms offer financial aid programs, and some insurance plans cover online therapy with minimal copays. Community mental health centers also offer sliding scale fees based on income.

Which online therapy platform is best for couples?

ReGain is a subscription couples platform from the BetterHelp family, designed so you can start alone or share an account with your partner. Ours is a couples-only service that matches you with a licensed couples therapist for video sessions and charges per session or by package; check its current prices before you book. Coverage for couples therapy varies: Talkspace says many insurance plans cover it, while ReGain does not take insurance at all.

How to Stress-Test a Platform Before Committing

Before paying for a full month of subscription or scheduling a stack of weekly insurance-billed sessions, run this short stress-test:

  1. Find their licensing claim. Every legitimate platform displays state licensure for therapists. If you can't find it, that's a red flag.
  2. Read the cancellation policy. Specifically: how much notice to cancel without being charged, refund policy on prepaid months, and what happens to your records.
  3. Read the privacy policy. Look for advertising-data sharing, third-party trackers, and what happens to session content. See telehealth privacy.
  4. Check whether it works in your state. Therapists must be licensed where you physically are during sessions. Cross-state licensure is real but not universal.
  5. Test the matching flow. Most platforms let you start the matching questionnaire without paying. If the question set feels generic, the match probably will too.
  6. Confirm what's actually included. "Unlimited messaging" might mean asynchronous-only. "Live sessions" might be 30 minutes, not 50. "Insurance accepted" might mean superbill-only.

What "Best" Doesn't Mean

A few honest caveats to rein in expectations:

  • Big network doesn't guarantee a great therapist for you. Network size affects probability, not outcome.
  • Fancy app doesn't equal good therapy. The clinician matters more than the interface.
  • Cheap doesn't necessarily mean accessible if the wait is long or the matching is poor.
  • Subscription isn't automatically cheaper than per-session insurance billing: do the math for your situation.
  • Insurance billing isn't always the cheapest path; high-deductible plans can make subscription competitive.
  • Online isn't always the right setting. For severe symptoms, complex psychiatric cases, or unsafe living situations, in-person care or higher levels of care are appropriate. See when telehealth isn't the right choice.

Specialty Use Cases Worth Knowing

A few specialty configurations come up enough to call out:

  • ADHD diagnosis and meds: telehealth-only ADHD platforms exist; insurance-billed psychiatric prescribers via Talkiatry, Headway, Grow Therapy, or Rula are usually a more sustainable path. See our note on when telehealth isn't right for ADHD prescribing concerns.
  • Eating disorders: most general-purpose platforms aren't equipped for severe eating-disorder care; specialized programs and IOPs are usually the right level. Look for evidence-based protocols (FBT, CBT-E) rather than generic counseling.
  • OCD: exposure and response prevention (ERP) is the evidence-based standard. Specialty platforms exist; on the major platforms, filter for OCD/ERP-trained therapists.
  • Trauma and PTSD: evidence-based options include CPT, PE, and EMDR. Filter platform searches for trauma-focused therapists; veterans should consider VA Video Connect first.
  • Substance use: telehealth-delivered IOPs and MAT are now widespread. Some specialty platforms (Monument, Workit) focus specifically on this.
  • LGBTQ+ affirming care: most major platforms allow filtering for LGBTQ+-affirming therapists; specialty directories also exist. See our broader guide.

What the FTC’s BetterHelp Case Means When You Compare Platforms

In July 2023 the Federal Trade Commission finalized an order against BetterHelp. The FTC alleged that the company gave customers’ email addresses, IP addresses and answers to its intake health questionnaire to Facebook, Snapchat, Criteo and Pinterest for advertising after promising to keep that information private. BetterHelp agreed to pay $7.8 million. The order bans it from sharing health data for advertising and requires consent before it discloses personal information to certain third parties.

Refunds went to people who signed up and paid for BetterHelp or its sister sites, including ReGain, Teen Counseling, Faithful Counseling and Pride Counseling, between August 1, 2017 and December 31, 2020. According to the FTC’s refund page, the first payments in June 2024 came to nearly $5.2 million, and a second round of more than $2.6 million went out in April 2025.

The point for any comparison: the sign-up questionnaire you fill out before you meet a therapist is not always handled like your sessions. Read a platform’s privacy policy, and check what it says about advertising partners, before you answer intake questions.

Final Word

Picking a platform is a starting move, not a destination. Most people who do well in online therapy switched therapists at least once, used the platform's messaging selectively rather than constantly, and gave the work a few months. The platform you start on isn't permanent. If something isn't working at week six, change the variable that's not working: sometimes the therapist, sometimes the format, sometimes the platform itself.
