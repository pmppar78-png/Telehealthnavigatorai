How We Compare Telehealth Services

Last updated October 22, 2026

Our “best” guides and comparison tables help you narrow down online therapy and psychiatry services. This page explains exactly where the figures in those tables come from, what we leave out, and where our method stops short, so you can judge how much weight to give them.

What we compare

  • Price: the self-pay price each company lists, with first-month discounts shown separately from the regular price.
  • Insurance: whether the company says it bills insurance, what it says insured members typically pay, and whether it gives receipts for out-of-network claims or accepts HSA and FSA cards.
  • Format: messaging, live video, session length and how many live sessions a plan includes.
  • Who provides care: licensed therapists, psychiatrists, nurse practitioners or non-clinical coaches, in the company’s own words.
  • Billing terms: how often you are charged, auto-renewal and what happens if you cancel early.
  • Public record: government actions involving a company, such as FTC orders or DEA rules that affect prescribing, taken from the agency’s own release.

Where the figures come from

Prices and plan details come from each company’s own pricing, FAQ or help-center pages. Rules and dates come from primary sources such as CMS, the DEA, the Federal Register, HHS and state licensing boards. We link the page we used next to the figure or in the guide’s Sources section, so you can check it yourself.

Every price table shows the date we checked it. When a company does not publish a figure, such as a self-pay price that is only quoted after sign-up, the table says so instead of estimating one.

What we do not do

  • No hands-on testing. We do not sign up for services, attend sessions or report on what a session felt like. Some larger review sites do this; we don’t, and we won’t imply that we have.
  • No user surveys or star ratings. We don’t publish scores, because a single number would hide the trade-offs that matter most, such as insurance status or who provides care.
  • No commissions. As of October 22, 2026, no link on this site is an affiliate link and no company pays us to be listed or ranked. Our affiliate disclosure explains how any future paid link would be marked.

How we decide what to include

A service appears in a comparison table only if we could confirm its price or insurance terms on its own site on the check date. If we couldn’t, we name the service in the guide and explain what was missing, rather than filling the gap with a figure from a third-party review.

Order in a table is not a ranking. Our guides describe who each service tends to suit, because the right choice depends on your insurance, budget and what kind of care you need.

Limits of this method

  • Prices and insurance networks change, sometimes weekly. The date on a table tells you how fresh it is; confirm the current price at sign-up.
  • A company’s own pages describe what it offers, not how well it delivers. For quality, ask about a provider’s license and training, and check it with your state licensing board.
  • What you pay with insurance depends on your plan, deductible and network. Call your insurer before your first visit.

Who does this work, and how to report an error

Guides are researched and written by our editor, Paul Paradis, who is not a clinician. Our behavioral health reviewer is listed on the reviewer page, and our wider process is described in our editorial standards.

If you spot an outdated price or a wrong detail, please contact us. We check the source and correct the page, along with its date.

If you are in crisis or may hurt yourself or others, call or text 988 or call 911.
